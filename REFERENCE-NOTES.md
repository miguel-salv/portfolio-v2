# Portfolio reference study

The shortlist came from [Awwwards’ developer collection](https://www.awwwards.com/websites/developer/). The actual websites were opened, scrolled, and inspected. The selection deliberately spans an editorial designer portfolio, a physical product company, a motion studio, and a hardware presentation. Subject matter alone does not determine a useful reference.

One composition governs the result: Gil Huybrecht. Seasats and Warm & Fuzzy contribute two specific disciplines within that composition. USAvionix is a comparison, not a fourth visual language. All portfolio images, models, project facts, career information, and identity remain Miguel’s.

## Primary: Gil Huybrecht

[Homepage](https://gilhuybrecht.com/) · [Profile](https://gilhuybrecht.com/profile) · [Awwwards entry](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/gil-huybrecht)

- **Composition and type:** The homepage places compact information in aligned columns above an image contact sheet. The profile uses an unusually large, plain grotesque paragraph, with an indented beginning and very little supporting decoration. The portfolio reproduces the hierarchy: a broad Archivo headline, a small factual introduction, a shared outer grid, and compact navigation. About gives the existing engineering biography a large paragraph opposite the portrait. Archivo is self-hosted; it serves both display and reading text, with the existing mono restricted to measurements.
- **Projects and images:** His image titles and sequence numbers sit above rectangular images, outside their boundaries. The new three-column engineering index closely reproduces that caption/image relationship using the actual matcher, vehicle, and robot build photographs. Each image has the same 4:3 aperture, the same top baseline, a title above, and project context below. At 1440px, the shared outer gutter is 52px and column gap is 24px. On a phone the images stack in the same reading order.
- **Navigation and transitions:** Compact native labels and restrained active treatment carry wayfinding. Miguel’s header keeps its existing links, search, theme switch and mobile menu. The chapter rail uses a clear blue current label and underline. The three engineering cards retain the existing 560ms image-to-case-study FLIP transition, including their current crops and white balance. Keyboard activation follows the same transition; reduced motion and modified clicks retain ordinary navigation. Gil’s alternate Star Wars presentation is not part of the portfolio direction.
- **Hover and focus:** Image emphasis remains a small, bounded enlargement. Text links use underlines or color, with a visible keyboard outline. Cards stay real links; wake and chase controls remain separate buttons. The active chapter has a persistent treatment rather than depending on hover.
- **Pacing:** An image-dense catalogue follows the quiet hero; the interactive mechanism occupies a larger space; the two playful builds form another compact gallery; the charcoal biography and flat career disclosures conclude the story. This makes the varied work read as one edited collection.

## Supporting: Seasats

[Website](https://www.seasats.com/) · [Awwwards entry](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/seasats)

- **Hero, scale and spacing:** The hero and Lightfish passage give a complete physical object a large, uninterrupted field. The portfolio closely reproduces that relationship with the complete matcher below the broad headline and to its right. The real C3 capacitor remains central and visible. The hero uses a static image, as required. On a phone, the whole object sits between the headline and introduction.
- **Project presentation:** Seasats places concise explanation and specifications beside the object, then uses documentary footage to prove deployment. The matcher section uses the same left-copy/right-object arrangement on spacious desktops, with measured results directly beneath the explanation. “In the lab” replaces the object aperture with the deployed build photograph. The engineering index gives those real builds equal visual authority before visitors enter the model sequence.
- **Navigation and transitions:** Its compact product selector informed the three hardware chapter labels. The portfolio retains opaque directional object handoffs with fixed readable text. Poster-to-film swaps and chapter-edge changes have no fades. The full Seasats sidebar is not needed for five projects.
- **Hover and focus:** Plain labels identify selectable modes and components. A persistent underline identifies Machine, Inside, Tune or In the lab; component selection also changes the explanatory text. Focus outlines remain visible, and keyboard input reaches each mode and component.
- **Scroll pacing:** Passive exploration reveals the matcher mechanism before moving into the vehicle and robot. At widths of 1100px or below, short desktop windows, and reduced motion, the chapters use ordinary document flow so their copy and controls remain reachable. Tune occupies the same visual aperture with only its interactive 3D VSWR surface.

## Supporting: Warm & Fuzzy

[Homepage](https://www.warmnfuzzy.tv/) · [Work gallery](https://www.warmnfuzzy.tv/work) · [Awwwards entry](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/warm-fuzzy)

- **Hero and typography:** The site pairs very large, direct sans-serif statements with substantial visual material. This reinforces the primary reference’s plain typography and strong scale contrast. The portfolio uses its own compact sentence-case headings and hardware portrait rather than a full-screen showreel.
- **Project images:** The actual work gallery has large rectangular media, top-aligned columns, and captions outside the image. That pattern governs the Kirby pair: two equal 4:3 photographs on the same baseline, followed by titles, descriptions, context, project links, and controls. No vertical offset is used. The photographs retain the devices’ real materials and environments.
- **Navigation and transitions:** Straightforward work navigation and clear link treatment keep the gallery usable. The portfolio keeps native project links and existing navigation rather than importing a separate gallery UI.
- **Hover and focus:** Warm & Fuzzy’s work responds through the media itself. The portfolio follows that principle: the companion wakes in its photograph, and the keychain lights on its PCB. The same actions have explicit keyboard-accessible controls and respect reduced motion. Its idle sticker/cube screensaver would compete with the engineering work and is not reproduced.
- **Pacing:** A quiet statement gives way to concentrated visual work. The portfolio follows that change in density, then uses a neutral charcoal biography as a deliberate pause after the playful photographs. No extra moving headline or unrelated effects were added.

## Comparison: USAvionix

[Website](https://www.usavionix.com/) · [Awwwards entry](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/usavionix)

- **Hero, scale and type:** Its three-line heading and complete diagonally placed aircraft demonstrate how a physical object can lead a technical homepage. It confirmed that the matcher could be much larger while retaining a full silhouette. Seasats already supplies that discipline, so no separate USAvionix hero layout was layered in.
- **Project presentation:** Compact specification callouts, exploded views and mission photographs link the object to evidence. This supported the decision to retain component inspection and measurable results alongside real build photographs.
- **Navigation and transitions:** Short chapter labels and passive object progression provide a useful comparison for the hardware rail. The portfolio keeps its established three-chapter interaction and opaque handoffs rather than importing a new navigation system.
- **Hover and focus:** Small controls belong near the object they affect. The portfolio’s mode buttons and component labels follow that locality, with its own persistent state and focus treatment.
- **Pacing:** Large object passages precede dense specifications and deployment imagery. This confirmed the quiet-object/dense-evidence rhythm selected from Seasats. Its terrain and military interface language are not part of Miguel’s identity.

## Remaining visual limits

The documentary photographs now receive small display-only white-balance corrections. The vehicle and robot covers use tighter crops while retaining their complete mechanisms. The same correction follows each Kirby photograph through its off, awake, and LED states. Original source photographs remain intact.

The vehicle and robot films have been rendered again from their existing scenes with differentiated rubber, metal, polymer, PCB, and plywood materials, broad neutral lighting, and softer native contact shadows. Geometry, object animation, camera orbit, original CAD, and older render assets remain intact. The temporary render scenes scale the lens values by 1.35 to match the existing catalogue framing; source scenes and their camera keys remain untouched. Both landscape and phone films retain their full loop durations and transparent backgrounds.

The original photographic environments and lighting still vary; a consistent new photo session would raise the quality further. The vehicle and robot remain photo-referenced reconstructions with simplified geometry. Their improved lighting does not add missing CAD detail.
